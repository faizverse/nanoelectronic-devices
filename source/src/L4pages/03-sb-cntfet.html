What you are seeing

Left. At each metal contact the bands are pinned: the conduction band starts a Schottky-barrier height φBn above the metal's Fermi level. Inside the tube the gate moves the bands. The bands bend from the contact to the channel over a screening length λ, set by the gate insulator. A thin barrier can be tunnelled through.

Ambipolar. A high gate voltage pulls the conduction band down, thinning the barrier for electrons at the source. A low gate voltage pushes the bands up, thinning the barrier for holes at the drain. With midgap contacts both happen, and the transfer curve is V-shaped.

Doped contacts replace the metal–tube barrier by n⁺ extensions: the device becomes a MOSFET-like, one-way transistor whose gate lowers a barrier in the channel.

Try this

  1. With midgap contacts, sweep the gate from −1 to +1.2 V. Where is the current smallest? Why at about VD/2?
  2. Make the tube 2.0 nm wide, then 1.0 nm. How does the minimum current change? (More detail compares three diameters.)
  3. Increase λ (a thicker gate insulator). What happens to the slope of the curve?
  4. Choose Pd-like contacts. Which carrier now flows at VG = 0? Then choose doped contacts and compare the off-current.

Check yourself

What does the gate change in a Schottky-barrier transistor?The width of the contact barriers, not their height. The height is set by the metal and the tube; the gate sets how quickly the bands bend away from the contact, and so how thin the barrier is.
Why do wide tubes leak more?The off-current is the smaller of the electron and hole tunnelling currents at the best gate voltage. Both barriers are about half the gap high, and the gap falls as 1/d, so the minimum current rises roughly as e−Eg/2kBT.
Why does a thin, high-κ insulator help?It shortens λ, so a small change in gate voltage thins the barrier a lot: steeper switching and more on-current.

For the curious: the model behind it

Midgap energy along a tube of length L = 20 nm: Emid(x) = Ech + (ES − Ech)e−x/λ + (ED − Ech)e−(L−x)/λ, with Ech = −qVG + flat-band offset (an ideal gate). Landauer current I = (4q/h)∫𝒯(E)[fS − fD]dE, with WKB transmission 𝒯 = exp(−2∫κ dx) and, inside the gap, κ = √(Δ² − (E − Emid)²)/ħvF, Δ = Eg/2, which describes electrons near the conduction band and holes near the valence band with one formula. Doped contacts: thermionic ballistic current over a gate-controlled barrier with threshold 0.25 V.

Left out: charge in the tube (the electrostatics are fixed, not self-consistent), image-force lowering, phonon scattering, contact resistance beyond the barrier, and band-to-band tunnelling in the doped device.
