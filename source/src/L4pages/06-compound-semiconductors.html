What you are seeing

Velocity–field. At low fields the velocity is mobility × field, so light-electron materials (GaAs, InGaAs) are far faster than silicon. In GaAs and InGaAs the velocity peaks at a few kV/cm and then falls: hot electrons move into heavy "satellite" valleys (the transferred-electron, or Gunn, effect). At high fields everything saturates, and the wide-gap materials SiC and GaN saturate highest.

Band-gap map. Alloys of two compounds have band gaps and lattice constants between those of their ends, so the gap can be tuned. A layer grows without defects only on a substrate with nearly the same lattice constant. A direct gap emits light at λ ≈ 1240/Eg nm.

Check yourself

Why does the GaAs velocity fall above about 4 kV/cm?Electrons gain enough energy (about 0.29 eV) to scatter into the L valleys, where their effective mass is much larger, so the average velocity drops. This negative differential mobility is the basis of the Gunn diode.
Why can AlGaAs be grown on GaAs at any composition?AlAs and GaAs have almost the same lattice constant (a difference of about 0.14%), so the alloy is lattice-matched to GaAs throughout.
Why do wide-gap materials matter for power electronics?Their critical field is about ten times silicon's, and the ideal on-resistance of a power device falls as the cube of the critical field. Their higher saturated velocity also helps at high frequency.

For the curious: the model behind it

Velocity–field: Si, SiC and GaN use v = μE/[1 + (μE/vs)β]1/β; GaAs and InGaAs use the transferred-electron form v = [μE + vs(E/E0)⁴]/[1 + (E/E0)⁴]. Parameters (μ cm²/V s, vs 10⁷ cm/s): Si 1400, 1.0; GaAs 8500, 1.0, E0 = 4 kV/cm; In₀.₅₃Ga₀.₄₇As 12000, 0.8, E0 = 3 kV/cm; 4H-SiC 900, 2.0; GaN 1000, 2.5. Alloys: linear lattice constants (Vegard's law) and band gaps with bowing parameters from Vurgaftman, Meyer and Ram-Mohan (2001); AlGaAs and GaInP switch from direct to indirect where their X valley falls below Γ.

Fits to typical room-temperature data for undoped material. The weak velocity peak of GaN, doping and alloy scattering, strain and temperature are left out.
