What you are seeing

Heavy doping, thin wall. Both sides are doped so heavily that the junction is only about 10 nm wide, and the Fermi levels (red dashes) sit inside the bands: the n side has a strip of filled states in its conduction band, and the p side a strip of empty states at the top of its valence band.

Filled must face empty. An electron can only tunnel into an empty state at the same energy. A small forward voltage lifts the n side's filled strip opposite the p side's empty strip, and electrons tunnel across (blue): the current rises to a peak.

Then it falls. More voltage slides the filled strip past the empty one, until it faces only the band gap, where there are no states. Tunnelling stops and the current falls: negative differential resistance. At larger voltages, electrons go over the step as in an ordinary diode (gold), and the current rises again.

Reverse. With reverse voltage, the p side's filled valence band faces the n side's empty conduction band, and a large current flows straight away.

Try this

  1. Start at 0 V. Raise the voltage slowly and stop at the peak. Which states face each other?
  2. Keep going until the current is at its lowest (the valley). What lies opposite the n side's filled strip now?
  3. Lower the doping. What happens to the peak current, and why? (Look at the junction width.)
  4. Switch from germanium to gallium arsenide. Why does the ordinary diode current start at a higher voltage?
  5. In More detail, turn the defect states off. What happens to the valley current?

Check yourself

Why does the current fall between the peak and the valley?Tunnelling needs filled states on one side facing empty states at the same energy on the other. As the voltage rises, the n side's filled strip is lifted past the top of the p side's valence band, so it faces the band gap, where there are no states. Fewer electrons can tunnel, so the current falls even though the voltage rises.
Why does lower doping give a much smaller peak?Lighter doping widens the junction (the wall) and shrinks the strips of filled and empty states. The tunnelling chance falls exponentially with the wall's width, so the peak current drops steeply.
Is the diode a source of power in its falling region?No. Current and voltage are both positive, so it still absorbs power from the battery. Only the changes have opposite signs, which is what lets it cancel losses in a resonant circuit and make an oscillator.

The Fermi levels sit inside the bands by an amount that grows with doping. The ordinary diode current is Is(eqV/kBT − 1) and the excess current through defect states is a small term that grows exponentially with voltage. Currents are scaled so that the most heavily doped germanium diode peaks near 10 mA.

Left out: the detailed shape of the band edges (band tails), the change of junction width with voltage, and series resistance. The shapes and trends are right; the numbers are typical, not those of a particular diode.
