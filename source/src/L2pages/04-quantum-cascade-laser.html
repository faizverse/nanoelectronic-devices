What you are seeing

Transitions inside one band. An ordinary laser diode emits when an electron falls across the band gap into a hole. A quantum cascade laser uses electrons only. In each stage the electron drops from an upper to a lower subband of the same conduction band, and the photon energy is set by the well width, not by the material's band gap.

Then it moves on. From the lower level the electron loses energy to the lattice (an optical phonon, about 34 meV) and passes through an injector region. If the bias is right, the injector level lines up with the upper level of the next stage and the electron tunnels straight into it, ready to emit again. One electron crossing N stages can emit up to N photons.

Alignment is everything. Too little bias and the injector sits below the next upper level; too much and it sits above. Either way tunnelling is blocked, the current falls, and lasing stops. The voltage across each stage must equal the photon energy plus the energy lost in relaxation and in the injector.

Try this in class

  1. Set the bias to zero, then raise it slowly. Watch the electrons stall at the injector until the green and rust levels line up; the current then jumps and the laser turns on.
  2. Overshoot the bias. The levels pass each other, the current drops — negative differential resistance — and the light goes out.
  3. Widen the active well from 6 to 10 nm. Predict: does the wavelength get longer or shorter? Where does the operating voltage move?
  4. Double the number of stages. The design voltage doubles, but so does the light per electron. Why is that a good trade?

Check yourself

Why can the same InGaAs/AlInAs materials make a 4 µm laser and a 9 µm laser?The photon energy is the spacing of two subbands, set by the well width. Changing layer thicknesses changes the wavelength without changing the materials.
Does "one electron, many photons" make QCLs very efficient?Not overall. Each photon is low in energy and the upper level empties quickly by phonon emission, so a high current is needed. Wall-plug efficiencies are typically 10–20%. The cascade raises the light per electron; the real advantage is the mid-infrared wavelength.
Why must the lower laser level empty quickly?Lasing needs more electrons in the upper level than in the lower one (population inversion). The design places the lower level one optical-phonon energy above another state, so electrons leave it within a fraction of a picosecond.

The model behind it

Each stage's active region is a single finite well (In0.53Ga0.47As in Al0.48In0.52As: m* = 0.043 m0, V0 = 0.52 eV). The photon energy is E2 − E1. The injector level sits 34 meV (one LO phonon) plus 60 meV below E1, so the stage aligns when the voltage per stage is

Vstage = (E2 − E1) + 0.034 + 0.060 eV

Tunnelling current is a resonance of width 20 meV around alignment plus a small leakage; lasing starts when the current passes a threshold, and output above threshold is proportional to N(J − Jth)(E2 − E1).

Left out: real active regions use two or three coupled wells and a graded superlattice injector, so their levels and alignment field differ. Currents and output are in relative units. The staircase is drawn to scale in energy, not in layer thickness.
