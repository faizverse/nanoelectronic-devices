What you are seeing

Places to sit. Think of the electrons as an audience filling a theatre with seats on many levels, one level for each energy. The blue curve is the number of seats on each level: the density of states. The rust area shows where the electrons actually sit. They fill the lowest levels first, and warmth spreads them a little higher.

Trapping reshapes the seats. In bulk material there are few places at the very bottom, and more as the energy rises. Trap the electrons in one direction (a quantum well) and the places come in a staircase. Trap them in two (a wire) and they bunch into sharp peaks. Trap them in all three (a dot) and only single sharp levels remain, like an atom.

Why it matters. The more directions are trapped, the narrower the range of energies the electrons occupy. A laser wants all its electrons at one energy, which is why quantum-well lasers need less current than bulk lasers, and why quantum-dot lasers are better still.

Try this

  1. Start with Bulk. See how the electrons (rust) spread over a range of energies.
  2. Switch to Well. The places now start with a step, and the electrons gather just above it.
  3. Try Wire, then Dot. Watch the places bunch up and the electrons squeeze into a narrower range.
  4. Make the trap smaller, then cool it down. In which case do almost all the electrons sit in the lowest level?

Check yourself

Is a quantum dot one-dimensional?No. A dot is zero-dimensional. The label counts the directions in which the electron is still free: a well is 2D, a wire 1D and a dot 0D.
Why does a quantum-well laser need less current than a bulk laser?In a well, a whole step of places is available right at the bottom, so the injected electrons gather at nearly the same energy and fewer are wasted at other energies.
Real quantum-dot lasers contain millions of dots. Why are their levels not perfectly sharp?The dots are not all exactly the same size, so their levels sit at slightly different energies. "More detail" has a slider for this size spread.

For the curious: the model behind it

GaAs-like electrons (m* = 0.067 m0) with perfect walls of width L in each trapped direction, so the level energies are sums of ħ²π²n²/(2m*L²). For each level, the density of states is

bulk ∝ √E,   well = m*/πħ² (constant),   wire ∝ 1/√(E − En),   dot: sharp lines

The electrons fill the places following the Boltzmann factor e−E/kBT. "More detail" shows the width of the occupied range (its full width at half maximum) for all four structures. Curves are scaled to the same peak height.

Left out: leaky walls (Animation 1 shows their effect), holes, and crowding at high electron density.
