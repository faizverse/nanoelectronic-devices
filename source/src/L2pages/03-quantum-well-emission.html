What you are seeing

A trap for both. A thin layer of GaAs sits between layers of AlGaAs. Electrons have a lower energy in the GaAs, and so do holes (the empty places left where electrons are missing). So the thin layer is a well that traps both.

They meet and give out light. An electron drops into a hole and gives out its energy as light. Because each is trapped in a box, each sits on a trapped level a little away from the bottom of its band. So the light carries slightly more energy than GaAs alone would give.

Thinner layer, shorter wavelength. Make the well thinner and both trapped levels move further out, so the light has more energy and a shorter wavelength. The designer tunes the colour by choosing the thickness. GaAs wells give near-infrared light, used in optical links, sensors and laser printers.

Check yourself

Why is the light from a quantum well bluer than from bulk GaAs?The electron and hole are trapped in a box, so neither can sit at the very bottom of its band. The light carries the band gap plus both of their trapped energies.
Why does the hole's level move less than the electron's?The hole behaves as a much heavier particle. In a box, a heavier particle has its energies closer together and nearer the bottom.
How can one material make lasers of different colours?By changing the thickness of the well. The thickness sets the trapped energies, and so the colour.

For the curious: the model behind it

Finite square wells for the electron (m* = 0.067 m0) and the heavy hole (0.50 m0). The barriers are AlxGa1−xAs with Eg = 1.424 + 1.247x eV; 65% of the extra gap goes to the electron wall and 35% to the hole wall. The photon energy is

Ephoton = Eg(GaAs) + E1e + E1hh

"More detail" lets you change the aluminium fraction, which sets the height of the walls, and shows the wavelength against thickness and how the photon energy is shared.

Left out: the electron–hole attraction (about 8 meV), strain and the light hole. The model matches measured GaAs/AlGaAs wells to within about 10 nm.
