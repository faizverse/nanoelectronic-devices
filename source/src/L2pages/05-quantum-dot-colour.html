What you are seeing

Size sets the colour. When a dot absorbs light it makes an electron and a hole. In a crystal smaller than the natural size of their bound pair (the exciton Bohr radius), both are squeezed by the walls and gain confinement energy that grows as 1/R². Their Coulomb attraction pulls the energy down, but only as 1/R. The Brus equation adds the two to the bulk band gap:

E(R) = Eg + (ħ²π²/2R²)(1/me* + 1/mh*) − 1.8e²/(4πε0εrR)

Small dots therefore glow blue and large dots red — within one material. The material's band gap is the floor: no dot can emit below it. CdSe covers the visible; PbS dots of the same size glow in the infrared.

The Brus equation overshoots. For dots a few nanometres across, the effective-mass picture breaks down: the bands are not parabolic so far from their edges and the walls are not infinitely high. The solid curve is the measured "sizing curve" used in laboratories; the dashed curve is Brus. They agree for large dots and part company below about 5 nm.

Try this in class

  1. CdSe, measured curve. Slide from 2 nm to 7 nm and call out the colours. Which size gives the green of a display (530 nm)?
  2. Switch to the Brus equation. At 3 nm it predicts violet-ultraviolet; the measured dot is green. How large is the error in eV?
  3. Change to PbS at the same 3 nm. The light is now infrared. Why, if the size is the same?
  4. Raise the size spread from 3% to 10%. The emission line broadens: why display makers care about uniform dots.

Check yourself

Why does the confinement term win over the Coulomb term in small dots?Confinement grows as 1/R² and Coulomb attraction as 1/R. Halving the radius quadruples the first but only doubles the second, so in small enough dots confinement dominates and the energy rises.
Why are PbS dots in strong confinement even at 8 nm?PbS has a very light electron and hole and a large dielectric constant, so its exciton Bohr radius is about 18–20 nm. Any dot smaller than that is strongly confined.
Why is the emission slightly redder than the absorption peak?The emitting state relaxes slightly after absorption (the Stokes shift, a few tens of meV), so emission sits just below the first absorption peak.

The model behind it

Brus (1984) with the parameters below; "measured" curves are empirical sizing curves: CdSe and CdTe from Yu et al., Chem. Mater. 15, 2854 (2003); PbS from Moreels et al., ACS Nano 3, 3023 (2009). Emission is drawn 30 meV below the first exciton, with a width set by the size spread through dE/dd.

CdSe: Eg 1.74 eV, me* 0.13, mh* 0.45, εr 10.6. CdTe: 1.50, 0.10, 0.40, 10.2. InP: 1.34, 0.077, 0.60, 12.5. PbS: 0.41, 0.085, 0.085, 17.2.

Left out: fine structure, surface and ligand effects, and non-parabolic bands (which is why Brus overshoots). InP has no simple published sizing curve, so only Brus is shown for it. Colours on screen are approximate.
