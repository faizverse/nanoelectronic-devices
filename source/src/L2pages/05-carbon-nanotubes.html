What you are seeing

Rolling up graphene. Graphene is a single sheet of carbon atoms in hexagons, like chicken wire. Roll it into a seamless tube about a nanometre across and you have a carbon nanotube. The arrow on the sheet shows which two points are joined when it is rolled. Two whole numbers, (n, m), describe the arrow, and so the tube.

Metal or semiconductor. How the sheet is rolled decides how the electron waves fit around the tube. If n − m can be divided by 3, the tube conducts like a metal. Otherwise it is a semiconductor, and the thinner the tube, the bigger its band gap. About one tube in three is metallic.

Why it matters. A transistor must be able to switch off, which needs a semiconducting tube. A metallic tube cannot be switched off and would short the transistor. Every growth method makes a mix, so tubes have to be sorted (Document 3, Section 5).

  2. Try the armchair tube (10, 10). Every armchair tube is metallic. Why, from the rule?

Check yourself

What decides whether a nanotube is a metal or a semiconductor?How the sheet is rolled, described by (n, m). If n − m is divisible by 3 it is metallic; otherwise it is a semiconductor.
Does a perfect metallic nanotube have zero resistance?No. Even with no collisions at all, it has about 6.5 kΩ, which comes from the contacts at its ends (Document 2, Section 5).
Why are nanotube chips so hard to make?One metallic tube can short a transistor, so almost every tube must be semiconducting. The tubes must also be lined up, in the right places, with good contacts.

For the curious: the model behind it

The roll is the chiral vector C = na1 + ma2, with |a| = 0.246 nm. Diameter d = (a/π)√(n² + nm + m²). Tubes with m = 0 are zigzag, those with n = m armchair, the rest chiral. The semiconducting gap, from the zone-folding model, is

Eg ≈ 2accγ0/d ≈ 0.8 eV·nm / d,   acc = 0.142 nm, γ0 ≈ 2.9 eV

"More detail" shows the gap of every semiconducting tube against diameter, the chiral angle and the size of the repeating unit.

Left out: curvature, which opens a tiny gap in non-armchair "metallic" tubes and shifts the gaps of the thinnest tubes. Tubes thinner than about 0.6 nm are too strained to be stable.
