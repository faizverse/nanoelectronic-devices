What you are seeing

Each n⁺/p junction carries a depletion region whose width grows with reverse bias:

W = √( 2εSi(Vbi + V) / qNA )

The drain junction is reverse-biased by VDS, so its depletion region spreads toward the source. While a strip of neutral p-type silicon remains between the two, electrons from the source face the full built-in barrier (≈ 0.9 eV) and essentially none cross below the surface.

When the two depletion regions touch — at the punchthrough voltage VPT — the drain's potential reaches the source directly. The barrier starts to fall with every extra volt on the drain, and current rises exponentially along a path below the surface where the gate has almost no control. Turning the gate further off does not help. Touching is the onset: the barrier has to fall by a few tenths of an eV before the bulk current overtakes the surface leakage, so the current takes off somewhat above VPT.

Try this in class

  1. Defaults (L = 250 nm, 10¹⁷ cm⁻³). Raise VDS slowly. Watch the drain depletion region grow and note the voltage at which it touches the source side.
  2. Go a little past VPT: the barrier in the middle plot drops and the current jumps by decades. Ask: what would you see on a curve tracer?
  3. Shorten the channel to 120 nm. VPT falls below a typical supply.
  4. Raise the body doping to 5×10¹⁷ cm⁻³. The depletion regions shrink and VPT recovers. That was the classical fix — and Document 3, Section 6 explains why it stopped working.

Check yourself

Why can't the gate stop punchthrough current?The current flows below the surface, at around the junction depth. The gate's field is screened by the inversion/depletion charge near the surface and cannot reach the deep path. Only the doping, or removing the deep silicon (SOI, FinFET, GAA), controls it.
Raising NA everywhere suppresses punchthrough. What does it cost?Higher VT and body factor, lower mobility (more ionised-impurity scattering), more junction capacitance and band-to-band leakage, and more random dopant fluctuation (Animation 11). Halo and retrograde implants put the doping only where it is needed.
Is punchthrough a different effect from DIBL?No — it is the terminal case of the same loss of electrostatic control. DIBL lowers the barrier at the surface; punchthrough removes it below the surface.

The model behind it

One-dimensional depletion approximation along a line at the junction depth, with the source at 0 V and the neutral body as reference. Before the regions meet, the potential is pinned at zero between them and the barrier for electrons is Vbi. After they meet, the region is fully depleted and

ψ(x) = Vbi + VDS·x/L − (qNA/2εSi)·x(L − x)

whose minimum sets the barrier Eb = Vbi − ψmin. The bulk current is I0·exp(−Eb/kT) with I0 = 1 mA/µm. Surface leakage uses VT = 0.4 V, SS = 85 mV/dec and DIBL = 40 mV/V.

Vbi = 0.9 V; L is the spacing between the junctions. Real punchthrough is two-dimensional and, once the barrier is gone, the current becomes space-charge limited rather than growing without bound.
