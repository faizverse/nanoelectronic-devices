What you are seeing

Where the gate overlaps the n⁺ drain, the voltage between them, VDG = VDS − VGS, appears across the thin dielectric. It depletes the drain surface and bends its bands upward. Once the bending exceeds the band gap (≈ 1.1–1.2 eV), the valence band at the surface sits above the conduction band a few nanometres deeper, and electrons can tunnel band to band: they go to the drain, while the holes left behind go to the body.

The tunnelling distance is about Eg/qEs, so the current depends exponentially on the surface field:

IGIDL ∝ Es · exp(−B/Es),   Es ≈ (VDG − 1.2 V) / (3 tox)

On the transfer curve this is a rising tail at negative gate voltage: driving the gate further off makes the leakage worse. GIDL sets a floor under the off-current that no amount of threshold engineering can remove, and in low-power and DRAM technologies it is often the limiting leakage.

Try this in class

  1. VDS = 1.2 V, EOT 2 nm. Move VGS from +0.3 V down to −1.5 V. Watch the bands bend until the gap is crossed horizontally.
  2. Find the minimum of the transfer curve. Ask: where would you set the off-state gate voltage?
  3. Thin the oxide to 1 nm: the same voltage gives twice the field, and the GIDL tail moves toward 0 V.
  4. Lower VDS to 0.7 V: GIDL drops by orders of magnitude. Discuss why it matters more for high-voltage I/O and DRAM than for core logic.

Check yourself

Why does a thinner gate dielectric make GIDL worse?For the same VDG, the field at the drain surface is roughly (VDG − 1.2 V)/(3tox), the factor 3 being εSi/εox. Halving tox doubles the field, and the tunnelling current depends exponentially on it.
Why does the band bending stop growing near 1.2 V?Once the bending exceeds the gap, holes generated by tunnelling (and thermally) collect at the surface and form an inversion layer. Further gate voltage is dropped across the oxide, raising the field rather than the bending.
Is GIDL a short-channel effect?Not directly — it happens in the gate–drain overlap and would occur in a long device too. Scaling worsens it through thinner dielectrics and steeper, more heavily doped junctions.

The model behind it

Depletion approximation in the n⁺ drain (ND = 10¹⁹ cm⁻³): VDG = ψs + 3toxEs with Es = √(2qNDψs/εSi), and the band bending ψs pinned at 1.2 V once reached (Chan et al., 1987). The bands are parabolic over the depletion width 2ψs/Es. GIDL current uses the Kane form with B = 21.3 MV/cm, scaled to a few nA/µm at VDG ≈ 2.7 V and 2 nm, and switched on smoothly as the bending passes the gap. Subthreshold current: VT = 0.35 V, SS = 80 mV/dec, DIBL = 50 mV/V.

Drain doping and the lateral field also matter in real devices; very heavy drain doping can suppress GIDL by preventing surface depletion. Trap-assisted tunnelling is left out.
