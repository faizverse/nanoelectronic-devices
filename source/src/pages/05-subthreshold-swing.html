What you are seeing

Below threshold, current is set by the barrier height, and every kT·ln10 = 59.5 mV of barrier is one decade of current at 300 K. The gate does not move the barrier one-for-one, though: it is one capacitor in a divider. The barrier potential follows the gate only by the fraction Cox/(Cox + Cd + Cdrain), so

SS ≈ (1 + (Cd + Cdrain)/Cox) · (kT/q) · ln10

In a long bulk device only the depletion capacitance Cd competes, giving 65–80 mV/dec. Shorten the channel and the source and drain couple to the barrier too (Cdrain), the gate's share falls, and the swing rises past 100 mV/dec.

The three short-channel symptoms then compound (left plot): the curve moves left (roll-off and DIBL) and flattens (swing), so the current at VGS = 0 climbs by orders of magnitude.

Try this in class

  1. Planar bulk, L = 100 nm: read SS (≈ 69 mV/dec) and note the gate's share in the divider.
  2. Slide L down to 25 nm. Watch the drain capacitor grow and SS pass 100 mV/dec. Read IOFF.
  3. Ask: with SS fixed at 60 mV/dec and VT = 0.3 V, what is the best possible ION/IOFF per 0.3 V? (5 decades.) Then compare with the readout.
  4. Switch to FinFET and GAA at the same L. The depletion capacitor vanishes (fully depleted body) and the drain capacitor shrinks.
  5. Finally: no setting goes below 59.5 mV/dec. Ask why — and point to steep-slope devices (tunnel FETs, negative capacitance) as the only way past it.

Check yourself

Why can't any MOSFET beat 60 mV/dec at room temperature?Current over a barrier is set by the Boltzmann tail of the source electrons: it changes by 10× per kT·ln10 of barrier. Even with perfect gate control (n = 1) the barrier moves one-for-one with VGS, so 59.5 mV per decade is the floor. Beating it needs a different injection mechanism, such as band-to-band tunnelling.
A chip needs ION/IOFF = 10⁵ with VDD = 0.7 V. What is the largest SS it can tolerate if VT = 0.3 V?The off-current is set by the 0.3 V below threshold. Roughly 5 decades must fit into it, so SS ≤ 300/5 = 60 mV/dec — at the limit. That is why VT, and with it VDD, stopped scaling.
Why does the multi-gate body have almost no Cd?The thin body is fully depleted and is not tied to a substrate, so there is no depletion region whose edge moves with the gate. The body factor m approaches 1.

The model behind it

Scale-length model of Animation 3. The swing factor is n = m/η, where m = 1 + Cd/Cox is the long-channel body factor and η = ∂ψmin/∂ψL is how strongly the barrier top follows the gate:

η = 1 − [sinh((L−xm)/λ) + sinh(xm/λ)] / sinh(L/λ)

with xm the position of the barrier top. The divider drawing uses Cdrain/Cox = n − m, which is exactly the extra coupling that reproduces the computed swing.

EOT 1 nm; bodies 20 / 6 / 5 nm. Interface traps, which add their own capacitance in parallel with Cd, are left out.
