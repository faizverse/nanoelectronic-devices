What you are seeing

The source and drain are heavily doped and sit at a higher potential than the channel. Their influence leaks into the channel, decaying over a characteristic distance — the natural length:

λ = √( (εSi/εox) · tSi · tox / N )

The shading in the cross-section shows that influence. In a long channel the two shaded zones are far apart and the middle of the channel belongs to the gate. Shrink L toward a few λ and the zones meet: the source and drain now help the gate turn the device on, so VT falls. That fall is threshold roll-off.

The damage is not that VT is lower — you could compensate for that. It is that VT becomes a steep function of L (right-hand plot), so a nanometre of lithographic variation becomes tens of millivolts of threshold spread. The design rule is L ≳ 5λ to 10λ.

Try this in class

  1. Planar bulk, defaults. Move L from 100 nm down to 20 nm and watch the shaded zones merge. Note where L crosses the 5λ line.
  2. At L = 30 nm read the sensitivity: how much does a 1 nm gate-length error move VT?
  3. Pull the two levers one at a time: thinner oxide (EOT), then a thinner body. Each is a square root, so halving either only improves λ by 30%.
  4. Now change the number of gates: FinFET (N = 2), then GAA (N = 4). This is the lever the industry actually used.
  5. Ask: what minimum gate length does GAA allow at EOT = 1 nm and a 5 nm sheet? (≈ 10 nm — the saturation the 2022 IRDS roadmap projects.)

Check yourself

Why is changing the geometry (N) a stronger lever than thinning the oxide?λ ∝ √(tox), and EOT is close to its practical limit (≈ 0.8–1 nm) because of gate tunnelling. Body thickness is limited by mobility loss and variability. N enters the same square root but jumps by 2× and 4× with no thinning of anything.
Roll-off uses VDS = 0.05 V. Why does the high-VDS curve roll off earlier?That is DIBL: the drain at high bias reaches further into the channel and lowers the barrier more. The gap between the two curves is DIBL × ΔVDS — Animation 3.
Textbooks explain roll-off by "charge sharing". Is that wrong?It gives the right trend and explains why shallow junctions help, but it is a one-dimensional story with no drain-voltage dependence, so it cannot explain DIBL and underestimates roll-off below about 50 nm (Document 3, Section 1.2).

The model behind it

The same scale-length model as Animation 3. The surface potential at threshold is

ψ(x) = ψL + (Vbi − ψL)·sinh((L−x)/λ)/sinh(L/λ) + (Vbi + VDS − ψL)·sinh(x/λ)/sinh(L/λ)

and VT is the gate voltage at which its minimum reaches the long-channel threshold value. For L ≫ λ the shift falls off as exp(−L/2λ). The shading is (ψ(x) − ψL) normalised to its value at the junctions.

Body factor for bulk: m = 1 + (εSi/tSi)/(εox/EOT); about 1.03 for fully depleted multi-gate bodies. Vbi = 1.1 V; long-channel VT = 0.30 V. No halo doping — real bulk devices used halos to flatten the roll-off curve, at the cost of other problems.
