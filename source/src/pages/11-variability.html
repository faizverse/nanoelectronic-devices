What you are seeing

The threshold voltage of a doped-channel MOSFET depends on the depletion charge under the gate — that is, on the dopant atoms there. In a large device the count is huge and averages out. In a nanoscale device it is not: a 15 × 15 nm channel doped at 10¹⁸ cm⁻³ holds fewer than ten atoms in its depletion region. Their number is Poisson-distributed and their positions random, so VT varies from device to device:

σVT ≈ (q/Cox) · √( NAWdm / 3WL )  ∝  1/√(WL)

The 1/√(WL) law is Pelgrom's law, written σVT = AVT/√(WL). An atom near the surface counts for more than one deep in the depletion region, which is where the factor 3 comes from.

The fix the industry adopted is radical: leave the channel undoped (FinFET, nanosheet, fully depleted SOI) and set VT with the gate metal's work function. Random dopant fluctuation then largely disappears; line-edge roughness and metal-grain variation remain.

Try this in class

  1. Start at W = L = 200 nm. Fabricate: thousands of atoms per device, a narrow histogram.
  2. Shrink to 20 × 20 nm and fabricate again. Count the atoms in each sample device and read σVT.
  3. Look at the ±5σ lines: with 10¹⁰ transistors on a die, devices that far out exist. Ask whether a 6-transistor SRAM cell still works if one of its transistors is 5σ off.
  4. Halve the oxide thickness: σ falls (q/Cox shrinks). Then press Undoped channel: the dopant spread vanishes, leaving the other sources.

Check yourself

Why does an atom near the surface affect VT more than a deep one?The gate couples to a charge through the oxide plus the silicon above it. A charge at depth y in a depletion region of width Wdm is screened partly by the body, and its effect on VT falls roughly as (1 − y/Wdm).
Doubling both W and L — what happens to σVT?The area quadruples, so σVT halves. That is why analogue designers use large matched devices, and why minimum-size SRAM transistors are the worst case.
If RDF disappears in undoped channels, why is variability still a problem?Line-edge roughness turns into gate-length variation, which roll-off converts into VT spread; metal-gate grains have different work functions; fin width and sheet thickness vary. Typical AVT improved, but devices also became smaller.

The model behind it

For each device the dopant count in the volume W × L × Wdm is drawn from a Poisson distribution with mean NAWLWdm, and each atom gets a random depth y. The threshold is

VT = VT0 + (q / CoxWL) · [ Σ(1 − yi/Wdm) − mean ]

whose standard deviation reproduces the formula above (Stolk et al., 1998). Wdm = √(4εSiφF/qNA); for the undoped option the body is a 6 nm fin with 10¹⁵ cm⁻³ background doping. The optional "other sources" term adds an independent Gaussian of 0.8 mV·µm/√(WL) for line-edge roughness and metal-gate granularity.

VT0 = 0.35 V. Atom positions along the channel are drawn but do not enter the threshold (a fuller treatment needs 3-D simulation; percolation through low-doped paths makes real distributions slightly skewed).
