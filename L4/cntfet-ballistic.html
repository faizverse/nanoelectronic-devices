The ballistic nanotube transistor

In a short nanotube, electrons cross from source to drain without scattering. The current is set by how many states at the top of the barrier are filled from each end. Change the gate, the drain, the tube and the insulator, and watch the quantum capacitance limit the charge.

What you are seeing

Left. The energy of the first subband at the top of the barrier in the channel (black line), set by the gate. Electrons moving towards the drain come from the source and fill states up to the source Fermi level μS; those moving back come from the drain, filled up to μD = μS − qVD. The current is the difference.

Right. The transfer curve. Below threshold the gate lowers the barrier and the current rises tenfold every 60 mV at 300 K. Above threshold the current rises roughly linearly, at most 4q²/h = 155 µA per volt for one subband.

The quantum capacitance. Adding electrons to the tube costs energy because its density of states is finite. With a thin high-κ gate, the gate moves the bands almost one-for-one, and the charge, and so the current, is set by the tube's own states.

Try this

  1. At VD = 0.5 V sweep the gate. Read the swing below threshold. Now raise the temperature to 400 K.
  2. Switch the insulator from SiO₂ to HfO₂ and thin it from 10 nm to 2 nm. When does the on-current stop rising? (More detail shows why.)
  3. Lower VD to 0.05 V, then raise it. Why does the current saturate after only about 0.1 V?
  4. Make the tube wider. What happens to the second subband, and to the current at high gate voltage?

Check yourself

Why does the current saturate without pinch-off?Once μD is a few kBT below the barrier top, the drain no longer fills any left-moving states, so the current is set by the source alone and stops depending on VD.
Why does making the insulator thinner eventually stop helping?The gate drives the insulator capacitance and the quantum capacitance in series. Once Cins ≫ CQ, the series combination is limited by CQ, which depends only on the tube.
What is the most current one subband can carry?4q²/h ≈ 155 µA per volt of overdrive: four channels (two spins, two valleys), each carrying q/h per unit energy.

For the curious: the model behind it

Top-of-the-barrier model (Natori; Rahman, Guo, Datta and Lundstrom, 2003). Electron density at the barrier n = ∫D(E)[f(E + U − μS) + f(E + U − μD)]/2 dE, with hyperbolic subbands and D(E) = (8/hvF)E/√(E² − Δ²). Self-consistent potential U = −q(αGVG + αDVD) + q²n/CΣ, with Cins = 2πε/ln[(2tox + d)/d], αG = 0.97, αD = 0.03. Current I = (4qkBT/h)·q[ln(1 + eηS) − ln(1 + eηD)] per subband. Two subbands; γ0 = 2.7 eV. The threshold is set by a fixed band offset (doped, ohmic contacts).

Left out: contact resistance (at least 6.45 kΩ per tube in reality), phonon scattering (real devices reach 50–80% of this ballistic current), band-to-band leakage and interface traps.
