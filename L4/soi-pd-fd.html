What you are seeing

Left. At threshold the gate depletes the film to a depth xd,max = √(4εSiφF/qNA). If the film is thicker, a neutral body is left underneath, connected to nothing: partially depleted (PD). If it is thinner, the depletion reaches the BOX: fully depleted (FD).

Right. In an FD film the front and back interfaces are coupled through the film, so the substrate acts as a second gate. Its voltage shifts the front threshold along the Lim–Fossum line, until the back surface inverts or accumulates and screens it. In a PD film the neutral body screens the back gate completely.

More detail. In a PD device, holes from impact ionisation collect in the floating body, raise its potential and lower VT: the current jumps, the "kink".

Try this

  1. Start at 150 nm and 10¹⁷ cm⁻³ (PD). Thin the film until it becomes fully depleted. Compare the thickness with xd,max.
  2. Choose the 28 nm FD-SOI preset (7 nm film, 25 nm BOX). Sweep the back gate. How many mV/V? Now set a 145 nm BOX.
  3. Compare the body factor n and the swing for PD and FD.
  4. With More detail on, raise the drain voltage in a PD device and find the kink. Switch on a body tie.

Check yourself

What is the condition for full depletion?tSi < xd,max. If both interfaces are depleting the film, a neutral layer survives only if tSi > 2xd,max.
Why does the back gate shift the threshold only in FD-SOI?In a PD film the neutral body sits between the back interface and the channel and screens the back gate. In an FD film there is no neutral layer: the field from the back gate reaches the front channel through the depleted silicon.
Why does a thin BOX give stronger coupling?The coupling coefficient is CSiCob/[Cof(CSi + Cob)] ≈ tof/tBOX for a thin film, so a thinner BOX couples more strongly.

For the curious: the model behind it

φF = (kBT/q)ln(NA/ni), xd,max = √(4εSiφF/qNA). FD: ∂VTf/∂VGb = −CSiCob/[Cof(CSi + Cob)] (Lim and Fossum, 1983), applied over the back-gate range in which the back surface potential runs from 0 (accumulation) to 2φF (inversion), a range of 2φF(1 + CSi/Cob), here centred on VGb = 0 with VTf = 0.40 V for reference. Body factor n = 1 + CSiCob/[Cof(CSi + Cob)] (FD) or 1 + Cdep/Cof (PD), swing = n·ln(10)kBT/q. The kink is a smooth step in the body potential above a drain voltage of about VDsat + 0.5 V; illustrative only.

Left out: quantum confinement in very thin films, the fixed charges and work functions that set the absolute VT, short-channel effects and self-heating.
