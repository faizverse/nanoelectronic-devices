FinFET and nanosheet: width and control

What you are seeing

FinFET. Each fin conducts along both sidewalls and its top, so its effective width is 2Hfin + Wfin. Fins sit on a pitch P, so the width packed into each µm of layout is (2Hfin + Wfin)/P, which exceeds 1 when fins are tall and close together. Width comes only in whole fins.

Nanosheet. Each sheet is surrounded by the gate, so it conducts on all four faces: 2W + 2T per sheet. The sheet width W is a free design choice, so there is no "whole fin" restriction, and stacking three or four sheets gives a lot of width from one footprint.

Control. A thin fin or sheet, gated from two or four sides, has a short natural length λ, so short gates still switch steeply. More detail shows the swing and DIBL from the same model as Lecture 1.

Check yourself

How can a FinFET have more width per µm than a planar transistor?Because its channel is vertical: each fin contributes 2Hfin + Wfin of width but occupies only one fin pitch of layout. With H = 46 nm, W = 7 nm and P = 34 nm, that is 99/34 ≈ 2.9 µm per µm.
Why does a thinner fin give better short-channel control?The natural length λ ∝ √(tSi·EOT/N) falls as the body thins, so the drain's field dies away faster along the channel. Keeping L ≳ 5λ keeps the swing near 60 mV/dec and the DIBL small.
What problem of FinFETs does the nanosheet solve?Fin quantisation: width comes only in whole fins. A nanosheet's width can be chosen continuously, and the gate surrounds the channel completely.

For the curious: the model behind it

Effective width: FinFET Nfin(2Hfin + Wfin), footprint Nfin·P; nanosheet Nsheet(2W + 2T), footprint W + stack spacing. Electrostatics: the Lecture 1 natural-length model, λ = √((εSi/εox)·tSi·EOT/N), with tSi = Wfin and N = 2 for the fin, tSi = T and N = 4 for the sheet; swing and DIBL from the quasi-two-dimensional potential along the channel. Intel fin dimensions are from the IEDM papers for the 22, 14 and 10 nm processes.

Left out: corner rounding, quantum confinement (which raises VT in very thin bodies), the top gate of a tri-gate fin, parasitic resistance and capacitance, and self-heating.
