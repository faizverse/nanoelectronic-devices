What you are seeing

Electrons in silicon already move at about 10⁷ cm/s at room temperature, in random directions. A field adds a small drift on top: v = μE. At low field the drift is tiny compared with the thermal motion — watch the cloud barely creep.

Raise the field and the electrons gain energy faster than they can lose it to acoustic phonons. Once they are energetic enough to emit optical phonons (≈ 63 meV in Si), every extra bit of energy is shed almost immediately. The drift velocity levels off at vsat ≈ 10⁷ cm/s — about the thermal velocity itself.

A 0.7 V supply across a 20 nm channel is an average field of 3.5 × 10⁵ V/cm, well into saturation. The current is then set by charge × vsat, not by mobility × field:

ID,sat ≈ W Cox vsat (VGS − VT) — linear in overdrive, independent of L

Try this in class

  1. Set the field to 10³ V/cm. The electrons jitter but hardly drift; the readout gives a drift velocity two orders below thermal.
  2. Raise it to 10⁴, then 10⁵ V/cm. The drift catches up with the thermal motion and then stops growing. Compare the bulk and inversion-layer curves: the surface lowers μ but not vsat.
  3. Now the device: move L from 2 µm down to 20 nm and watch the saturation current. Long channels follow 1/L; short ones flatten out.
  4. At L = 30 nm, double the overdrive. The current roughly doubles instead of quadrupling. Ask what that does to gm (it becomes ≈ W·Cox·vsat, a constant).

Check yourself

If current no longer improves with shorter L, why keep scaling L?Density and capacitance. A shorter gate means a smaller transistor and less gate capacitance to charge, so switching energy and delay still improve even when drive current per width does not. (And in very short channels quasi-ballistic transport lets the source injection velocity exceed vsat somewhat — Document 2, Section 4.1.)
Why does VDS,sat fall below VGS − VT in a short channel?The carriers at the drain end reach vsat before the channel pinches off. The current saturates as soon as the field there reaches the critical field Ec, which happens at a smaller VDS when L is short: VDS,sat → EcL.
Holes saturate at a similar velocity but have lower mobility. What does that imply for p-MOS?Holes need a higher field to reach saturation, so long p-MOS devices are weaker, but in very short channels the n/p gap closes partly, because both are capped near vsat. Strain engineering for holes (Document 1) was largely about raising effective mobility and injection velocity.

The model behind it

Bulk electrons (Caughey–Thomas, β = 2): v = μ₀E / [1 + (μ₀E/vsat)²]1/2, μ₀ = 1400 cm²/Vs, vsat = 1.07×10⁷ cm/s

Device model: v = μE/(1 + E/Ec) with Ec = vsat/μ, μ = 300 cm²/Vs and vsat = 10⁷ cm/s, so v → vsat at high field. Integrating along the channel gives

VDS,sat = 2Vov / (1 + √(1 + 2Vov/EcL)),   ID,sat = W Cox vsat (Vov − VDS,sat)

Below VDS,sat: ID = (μCoxW/L)(VovVDS − VDS²/2)/(1 + VDS/EcL), which joins the saturation value smoothly.

VT = 0.3 V, Cox = 2.5 µF/cm² (EOT ≈ 1.4 nm), W = 1 µm. The particle animation is schematic: drift is drawn to scale against a fixed thermal speed.
