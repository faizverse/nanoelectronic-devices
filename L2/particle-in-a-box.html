What you are seeing

Only certain notes fit. A guitar string pinned at both ends can only vibrate with a whole number of humps. An electron trapped in a box is the same: its wave must fit inside the box, so only whole numbers of half-waves are allowed. Each one has its own energy, and no energies in between are possible. The lowest is labelled n = 1, the next n = 2, and so on.

Smaller box, bigger energies. Squeeze the box and every wave must wiggle more tightly, so every energy goes up: halve the width and the energies go up four times. A lighter particle also has more widely spaced energies.

It never sits still. There is no n = 0, so the lowest energy is never zero.

Real walls leak. Choose "Real (leaky)" walls and the wave spills a little way into the walls. The energies drop slightly, and a shallow box can only hold a few of them.

Try this

  1. Start with perfect walls and a 10 nm box. Note the lowest energy. Predict what happens if you halve the width to 5 nm, then try it (it goes up four times).
  2. Switch from "the wave" to "where it is likely". In the n = 2 state, where is the electron never found?
  3. Switch to real, leaky walls. Do the energies go up or down? Look at the wave near the walls.
  4. Make the box very narrow, about 2 nm, with leaky walls. How many allowed energies are left?

Check yourself

Why can a trapped electron not have zero energy?A wave with no humps is no wave at all: there would be no electron. So the lowest allowed state, n = 1, always has some energy. Another way to see it: if the electron were perfectly still inside a small box, we would know both where it is and how fast it moves, which quantum mechanics does not allow.
Why do the energies go down when the walls leak?The wave spreads a little into the walls, so it has slightly more room. A wave with more room wiggles less tightly, and that means less energy.
Two quantum dots of the same material glow red and blue. Which is smaller?The blue one. A smaller box pushes the energies further apart, so the light given out has more energy, which means bluer light.

For the curious: the model behind it

Perfect walls: En = ħ²π²n²/(2m*L²), ψn = √(2/L) sin(nπx/L). Leaky (finite) walls: the even and odd matching conditions k tan(kL/2) = κ and −k cot(kL/2) = κ, with k = √(2m*E)/ħ and κ = √(2m*(V0−E))/ħ, are solved numerically and the exact waves are drawn. "More detail" shows the energies against width, the chance of finding the electron outside the box, and the spacing compared with the thermal energy kBT = 25.9 meV.

Left out: the change of effective mass at the walls, and strain. The trends and sizes of the energies are right.
